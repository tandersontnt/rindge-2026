How to read the labels

  • Official A town, state or federal document, such as approved minutes or an annual report.
  • Official · text-extracted · verify against PDF Official · scan, verify against PDF Numbers that software read from a PDF or scan. Check the original before quoting.
  • Official · draft minutes Posted but not yet approved.
  • Press-reported From a newspaper or a state or association summary.
  • Unofficial, from meeting audio Automated summary Context only, and approximate.
  • Derived / statutory Dates computed from state law. The town has not posted them.
  • Gap Withheld Not found, or held back because the printed figure looks wrong.

Town facts and history

Rindge, incorporated in 1768, is a town of about 6,500 people with many lakes and ponds on the Massachusetts border.

In plain language

Rindge was incorporated in 1768. It covers about 40 square miles on the Massachusetts border, has many lakes and ponds, and had 6,476 residents in the 2020 Census.

March 2026 election

Voters turned down the proposed town budget in March 2026, so the $5,654,177 default budget applies.

In plain language

In March 2026 voters turned down the proposed $5.81 million town budget (707-824), so the $5,654,177 default budget applies. All seven savings-fund articles passed. These results come from newspapers; the town has not posted official results.

March 2026 results are press-reported only; they are not yet in any annual report. The Deliberative Session was Sat Jan 31, 2026 Official and voting was Tue Mar 10, 2026 (7am–7pm, Rindge Memorial School). The town's results page returned an error on Oct 9, so these tallies are as reported by the Monadnock Ledger-Transcript and the Keene Sentinel. Both were re-checked online on Oct 9.

Rindge by the numbers

The key figures, including the 2025 tax rate of $13.79 per $1,000 and the elderly exemption count.

In plain language

About 6,500 people live in Rindge. After the 2025 revaluation the tax rate fell to $13.79 per $1,000, because assessed values roughly doubled. That does not mean tax bills were cut in half.

Budget and finances

The town spends about $5.5 to $5.8 million a year on its own operations, before school and county taxes.

In plain language

The town spends about $5.5 to $5.8 million a year on its own operations, before school and county taxes. Voters rejected the proposed budget in 2025 and 2026, so the default budget applies. The largest lines are police, highway and fire.

Department budgets, savings funds, debt and the capital plan, from the town's 2024 and 2025 annual reports. 2026 vote results are press-reported.

Trends, 2011–2026

How budget votes, zoning changes and tax rates have moved since 2011.

In plain language

Since 2011 voters have approved the town budget in most years but rejected it in 2011, 2021, 2025 and 2026. Zoning changes usually pass. Numbers read from scanned reports are marked so you can check them against the original.

Built from the town's annual reports (2011–2025), the town's board index pages and press reports for 2026. Every value is labeled. Values read from scans are marked "verify against PDF", and figures that look like printing errors are withheld.

Laws and regulations that apply in Rindge

Plain-language notes on Rindge's zoning, its regulations and the state laws behind them, each linked to the official text.

In plain language

Rindge's zoning ordinance sets lot sizes, setbacks and frontage (250 feet on a public road in the residential and village districts). State law sets how rules change: zoning changes and citizen petitions go to voters on the March ballot. This is a summary, not legal advice.

Summary, not legal advice. These plain-language notes point to the official text. Laws change; read the linked source and check with the Planning Department, Town Clerk or a lawyer before acting.

Emergency and utilities

In an emergency, call 911.

In plain language

In an emergency, call 911. Ambulance service is due to switch to Peterborough Fire & Rescue on January 1, 2027 (press-reported). No town water or sewer system is listed, so homes generally use private wells and septic (unconfirmed).

Schools and community

Rindge children attend the Jaffrey-Rindge Cooperative School District (SAU 47), whose budget is voted separately from the town's.

In plain language

Rindge children attend the Jaffrey-Rindge Cooperative School District (SAU 47): Rindge Memorial School for PreK–5, then Conant Middle and High School in Jaffrey. School budgets are voted separately from the town budget.
